Budgeting is deciding, ahead of time, what your money is for. That is all. It is not about spending less, or feeling bad about coffee. It is about knowing.

## The one question

Most money worry comes down to one question: **can we afford this?** Without a plan, the honest answer is "I don't know". The bank balance does not help much. It includes next week's rent and the insurance bill you forgot.

A plan answers the question. Once the bills and the savings are accounted for, what is left really is yours to spend.

## Four kinds of money

Almost every family's money falls into four groups. Noodle has a word for each.

- **What comes in.** Your pay after taxes. Noodle calls it Take-home pay.
- **What you have promised.** Rent or mortgage, loans, insurance, utilities, daycare, subscriptions. You signed up for these, and they come on a schedule. Noodle calls each a Commitment.
- **What you choose, day to day.** Groceries, fuel, eating out, hobbies. The amount changes every month, so you give each kind a monthly amount to stay inside. Noodle calls each a Bucket.
- **What you are building.** An emergency fund, a holiday, a paid-off card. Noodle calls each a Goal.

## What is left

Take what comes in. Subtract what you have promised, what you have set for day-to-day spending, and what you are putting toward Goals. The rest is **Free to Spend**: money with no job yet. You can spend it, or give it a job.

If the number is below zero, the plan promises more than comes in. That is useful to know on the first of the month, not the thirtieth.

## Why a plan beats tracking

Tracking tells you where money went. A plan tells money where to go. Tracking alone is a list of things you can no longer change. A plan lets you make the trade-offs before you spend: more for hockey this month means less for something else, and you choose which.

## It does not have to be exact

A plan is a best guess that you correct as you go. Some months a Bucket runs over. That is normal. You cover it from somewhere else and carry on. Over a few months the amounts settle near what your family really spends.

## A little, every week

A budget fails when it needs attention every day. Noodle asks for a few minutes once a week, in the Check-in. The rest of the time, one glance at Free to Spend is enough.
