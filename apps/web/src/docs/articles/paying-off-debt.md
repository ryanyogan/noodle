Paying off a credit card or a loan is one of the best things a Plan can do for a family. It also confuses people more than anything else in budgeting, because the same dollars seem to show up twice. This page explains the idea, then how Noodle handles it.

## The idea

There are two different situations, and they work differently.

**A card you use and pay.** You buy groceries on the card. That is the spending, and it is filed in Groceries on the day you buy. Later you pay the card from checking. That payment is not new spending. It is money moving between two of your own Accounts. If the payment counted too, the groceries would count twice.

**A balance you are carrying.** You owe money from before: a car loan, a student loan, or a card balance built up before your Plan began. Those purchases were never filed in your Buckets. Paying this down is a real cost to this month, and the Plan has to make room for it.

## Paying the card is a Transfer

When you pay a credit card from checking, Noodle calls it a **Transfer**. It goes in no Bucket.

When checking and the card are both in Noodle, the two sides are paired on their own. When only one of them is, Noodle asks in Review, and you say **It's a card payment**.

> If a card is not in Noodle at all, what you buy on it is not counted anywhere. Connect it or add it on Accounts so your Buckets show the whole picture.

## A regular payment is a Commitment

A loan payment, or a set monthly payment on a balance you are carrying, is a bill you expect every time. Make it a **Commitment**.

When you add or edit the Commitment, choose the card or loan under **Pays down**. Each payment filed in the Commitment then brings **what's owed** on that Account down. On the Commitment's page the charges read "Payments", with "Still owed" beside them.

1. Go to **Plan**, then **Commitments**.
2. Add the Commitment with its name, amount and schedule.
3. Under **Pays down**, choose the card or loan. If it is not in Noodle yet, choose **Add a card or loan…** and enter what is owed today.
4. Choose **Add Commitment**.

Once a card or loan is chosen, the form may offer an amount from your last three months of payments to it, for example "About $2,300 a month across 9 payments".

### A card Noodle already follows

If Noodle already sees what you buy on a card, because it is connected to its bank or you imported its purchases lately, paying it is a Transfer. You can still choose it under Pays down, but only by ticking **This is a set payment on a balance I'm carrying**.

If you connect such a card later, the Plan asks under **Things to check** whether to end the Commitment or keep it for a balance you are carrying. This stops its payments counting twice.

## Extra payments are a payoff Goal

To get a card or loan to $0 faster, add a Goal for it. In the app it is called **Pay off a card or loan**.

1. Go to **Goals** and choose **Add Goal**.
2. Under **What's it for?**, choose **Pay off a card or loan**.
3. Pick the card or loan. What is owed today becomes the target.
4. Add a **Paid off by** date if you like.

Each month you plan extra payments for it from Free to Spend. Then you pay the card or loan from checking as usual. The regular payment stays a Commitment. The Goal is the extra, on top.

The Goal shows **Still owed** and how much you have paid down. It follows the real balance.

- When the balance comes down, so does what is left to pay.
- New charges take the balance back up, and the Goal shows that honestly.
- At $0 it shows **Paid off**, and you complete it.

## Keeping what's owed up to date

What's owed is the balance as Noodle knows it.

- On a connected Account, the bank keeps it up to date.
- On an Account you keep by hand, each payment filed in a Commitment that pays it down comes off it.
- You can also enter it yourself. On a payoff Goal, choose **Update what's owed** and type the figure from the card's or loan's website.

> By hand, what's owed misses new charges and interest until you update it. Check it against a statement now and then.

## Good to know

- A card or loan can have one active payoff Goal.
- Nothing is Set aside for a payoff Goal. Set aside is for Goals that save.
- If new charges take the balance above where the Goal started, **Start again from today's balance** makes today's figure the new target.
- Not paying it off for now? Archive the Goal. What is owed stays as it is.
