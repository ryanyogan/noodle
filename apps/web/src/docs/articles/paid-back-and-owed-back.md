Sometimes you pay for something and someone else pays part of it back: a Child's other parent pays half of tuition, a Child pays for their own skates from their own account, a friend covers their share. That money is not Income and it is not a Refund. Noodle calls what they owe **Owed back**, and the money when it arrives **Paid back**.

## Say someone is paying part back

1. Open the purchase from **Transactions**.
2. Choose **Someone's paying part of this back**.
3. Under **Who's paying it back**, type a name, like Casey. Or pick one of the Children.
4. Under **How much**, leave half, or type the amount.
5. Choose **Save**.

The purchase now reads "Owed back $600 · Casey". On a split purchase you can say it for the whole purchase or for one Split.

## Let a Rule remember

When a Rule filed the purchase, Noodle offers **Remember "Tuition: Casey pays back half"**. Choose it, and the next tuition charge the Rule files gets its Owed back by itself. **Stop remembering** turns it off.

## When the money arrives

1. Open **Transactions** and find the money under **Money in**.
2. Choose **Change what … is**, then **Paid back**.
3. Noodle asks what it is paying back and offers the oldest purchases first. Change any amount.
4. Choose **Confirm**.

One payment can pay back several purchases. A purchase can stay partly owed. Money beyond what is owed waits as **Paid back, not matched yet** and is never counted as Income.

## Take a match off

Under the Paid back line, each purchase it was matched to has **Take off**. The purchase is owed again, the money waits, and you can match it somewhere else. A match that counted in a month that has ended stays.

## The Owed back list

**Transactions** has an **Owed back** list: each person, what they still owe, and the purchases it is for, oldest first.

## Where the money counts

Money Paid back counts in the month it arrives. It goes back into the Bucket or Commitment the purchase was filed in. A month that has ended never changes.

- Until the money comes, a bill someone shares reads "$600 over · $600 owed back by Casey".
- When it comes, the bill reads "$600 Paid back by Casey".
- A Bucket that got more back than it spent reads "$45 Paid back".

## An example: tuition

Tuition is a $600 Commitment: your half. In September the school charges the whole $1,200, and you also buy skates for $45 and pay the dentist $80.

1. On the tuition charge, say Casey is paying half back: "Owed back $600 · Casey". September's bill reads "$600 over · $600 owed back by Casey".
2. On the skates, say Casey owes $45. On the dentist, $80. Casey now owes $725.
3. In October Casey sends $700. Change it to **Paid back**.
4. Noodle offers $600 to tuition, $45 to the skates and $55 to the dentist. Choose **Confirm**.

Casey still owes $25 on the dentist. October's tuition reads "$600 Paid back by Casey", the skates' and the dentist's Buckets get $45 and $55 back, and none of it is Income. September stays as it ended.

## Money from a Child's own account

A Child's own account is not an Account in Noodle. It is the Child's money. Allowance you send there is spending: a Commitment, For that Child. When a Child pays you back for something, mark it Owed back by that Child, and the money is Paid back when it arrives.

## Good to know

- The person is a name, not a Member. They never see Noodle.
- What someone owes is a list, not a balance. It is only as complete as the purchases you marked.
- Money from the merchant is a Refund. Money from the other Parent is Between us.
