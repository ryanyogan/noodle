Most of what a family spends falls into two kinds. Some of it is promised: you signed up, and the bill comes whether you think about it or not. The rest is chosen, a little at a time, every day. Noodle keeps the two apart because they need different kinds of attention.

## The idea

A promised bill needs no willpower. It needs to be known about, so it is never a surprise.

Day-to-day spending is the opposite. No single purchase is a surprise, but together they can run away from you. What helps is a limit, and seeing how much of it is left.

So Noodle has two words.

- A **Commitment** is a bill you expect every time.
- A **Bucket** is a monthly allowance for one kind of day-to-day spending.

## What is a Commitment

Something you signed up to and pay on a schedule: rent or the mortgage, a car payment or other loan, insurance, electric, gas, water, trash, phone, internet, childcare and tuition, memberships and subscriptions.

A Commitment has an amount and a schedule: monthly, every two weeks, or yearly. Noodle takes what your Commitments are expected to cost out of the month before anything else.

## What is a Bucket

Spending that varies and that you decide as you go: Groceries, Fuel, Fun, eating out, clothes, a child's hockey.

A Bucket has an allowance for the month. Every purchase is filed in a Bucket, and the Bucket shows how much is left.

## A quick test

Ask two questions.

1. **Did we sign up for it?** A contract, a subscription, a loan or a membership points to a Commitment.
2. **Could we spend less on it this week by choosing to?** If yes, it is a Bucket.

Spending that only happens to repeat is still a Bucket. Coffee every morning, a weekly grocery shop and filling the tank are habits, not bills. They belong in Buckets, where you can see them against a limit.

## Some that are easy to mix up

- **Groceries.** Every week, but the amount is yours to choose. A Bucket.
- **A streaming subscription.** Small, but you signed up and it is the same every month. A Commitment.
- **Fuel.** Regular, but it varies. A Bucket.
- **Daycare.** A fixed bill. A Commitment.
- **Hockey.** The season's fee, if it is billed on a schedule, is a Commitment. Sticks, tape and tournament snacks are a Bucket.

## Yearly cost

Each Commitment shows what it costs in a year. A subscription of $15.99 a month is about $192 a year. Seeing the yearly figure makes it easier to decide whether something is still worth it.

## Lumpy months

Not every Commitment is due every month. A yearly bill lands in one month. A bill paid every two weeks usually comes twice a month, but now and then three times.

A month like that has less Free to Spend than usual. Noodle calls it a **Lumpy month**, and shows it ahead of time on the Plan's Year page, marked "Lumpy", so it is not a shock.

## How Noodle uses the two

- Your Take-home pay comes first. Commitments come out of it, then Buckets, then Goal funding. What is left is Free to Spend.
- This Month shows each Commitment as paid, partly paid or due, and each Bucket by how much is left.
- When Noodle spots a charge that keeps coming back at the same amount, it may suggest adding it as a Commitment. When it spots spending with no Bucket of its own, it may suggest a Bucket. You decide.

> If you are not sure, start with a Bucket. You can add a Commitment later, when you see the same charge come back.
